Our Services

What we build using CATIA

🔩
Part Design

Turning a sketch into a 3D part

What it is: We take a rough sketch or requirement and build a precise 3D digital model of the part in CATIA. This model shows exactly how the part should be shaped, sized, and manufactured.

Why you'd need it: This is the first step before anything can be manufactured. Without a precise 3D model, a factory can't know how to build it correctly.

Structural Design

Frames, ribs, and load-bearing structures

What it is: We design the structural frame — the skeleton — that holds a product together. For an aircraft, this means wing ribs, fuselage frames, and brackets. For a vehicle, it means the body structure and chassis supports.

Why you'd need it: Aircraft and vehicles need to be strong enough to withstand forces in use. The structure has to be designed precisely so it doesn't fail.

Learn More → Enquire for Details
⊞
Assemblies

Fitting hundreds of parts together

What it is: We take individual parts and assemble them into a complete design — the way an engine is made of hundreds of smaller pieces. This lets you see how everything fits before anything is built.

Why you'd need it: If parts don't fit together correctly, manufacturing problems appear too late and cost more to fix. Checking the assembly in CATIA first catches these issues early.

Enquire for Details
📐
Technical Drawings

The sheets a factory reads to build the part

What it is: From the 3D model, we create detailed technical drawing sheets — flat documents showing exact dimensions, tolerances (how much a measurement can vary), and notes a factory needs to manufacture the part correctly.

Why you'd need it: A 3D model isn't enough on its own — a manufacturing team needs drawing sheets that spell out every detail clearly.

Enquire for Details
⚡
Electrical Routing

Planning where wires run inside the structure

What it is: We design the routing of electrical wiring — called a wire harness — inside an aircraft or vehicle structure. In CATIA, we plan exactly where each bundle of wires travels through the structure, how it's secured, and where it connects.

Why you'd need it: Aircraft and vehicles have hundreds of wires running through them. Planning those routes in 3D before production prevents clashes with other parts and makes assembly faster and safer.
