Program 1
Program 1
● Easy

Getting Started with CATIA

Best for: complete beginners — no CATIA experience needed.

Learn the basics of building parts on-screen and assembling them together, then learn how to create the technical drawing sheets used to manufacture them. This is the foundation that every other program builds on.

What you'll learn to do:
  • Build a simple 3D solid part from scratch in CATIA
  • Put several parts together into a basic assembly
  • Create a flat technical drawing from the 3D model

Program 2
Program 2
● Medium

Advanced Part & Material Design

Best for: those who've completed the basics and want to go further.

Builds on Program 1 to cover more advanced material types used in aircraft. This includes composite materials (like carbon fibre — materials made of many thin layers bonded together for strength) and thin sheet metal parts (like aircraft skin panels — the thin metal sheets that form the outer surface of a wing or fuselage).

What you'll learn to do:
  • Design sheet metal parts that can be bent and formed
  • Design composite parts — layered materials used in aerospace
  • Understand how material choice affects design decisions

Program 3
Program 3
● Medium

Motion & Assembly Simulation

Best for: those interested in how parts move and fit together.

Covers everything in Program 1, plus two advanced areas: simulating how parts move (called kinematics — like checking whether a hinge or door opens without hitting anything), and simulating how a product is assembled step-by-step on a production line before it's physically built.

What you'll learn to do:
  • Set up a moving mechanism in CATIA and test its range of motion
  • Simulate how an assembly is built, step by step (process simulation)
  • Check for collisions between parts before manufacturing
Enquire for Details →
Program 4
Program 4
● Medium

Electrical Routing Design

Best for: those interested in wiring and electrical systems design.

Covers everything in Program 1, plus how to plan and design the routing of electrical wiring within a structure. A wire harness is the bundled set of wires and cables that carry electrical signals through an aircraft or vehicle — like the nervous system of the product. In CATIA, you plan exactly where each bundle runs, how it's clamped, and where it connects.

What you'll learn to do:
  • Route wire bundles through a 3D structure in CATIA
  • Set up connectors, clamps, and branch points correctly
  • Produce the documentation an electrical assembly team needs

After the Programs

What you'll be able to do

Together, these four programs cover the full range of CATIA skills used in aerospace and automotive engineering.

🔩

Build things

Design solid parts, thin sheet-metal parts, and layered composite parts — the three main types of parts used in aircraft and vehicles.

▶

Test things virtually

Simulate how parts move and how they'd be assembled, before anything is physically built. Catch problems early, on screen.

⚡

Wire things

Plan out electrical wiring paths within a larger design — the skill behind designing every aircraft's electrical system.

✈

Where these skills are used

These programs are designed around aerospace and automotive design — the industries that rely most heavily on CATIA. The skills transfer directly to roles at aircraft manufacturers, vehicle OEMs, and their supply chains.
