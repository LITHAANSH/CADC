Electrical Routing — planning where wires run inside the structure

We plan and design the routing of electrical wiring — called a wire harness — within aircraft and vehicle structures in CATIA. Think of it as designing the nervous system of the product.

What It Involves

Routing wires through complex 3D structures.

An aircraft or vehicle contains hundreds (sometimes thousands) of wires carrying power and signals between systems. These wires can't just be placed anywhere — they need to be carefully routed through the structure to avoid physical conflicts with other parts, follow safe paths, and be accessible for maintenance.

A wire harness is the collective term for a bundled set of wires, cables, and connectors — like the wiring loom under a car bonnet, but much more complex in an aircraft. In CATIA, we model the full 3D route of each harness through the structure, including where it's clamped, where it splits into branches, and how it connects at each end.

This work is also sometimes called "electrical layout" or "wire harness routing design." It's done in CATIA using specialised tools for electrical systems — the same tools we teach in Program 4 of our training academy.

What the work includes

⚡

Harness Routing

Defining the path each wire bundle takes through the structure — choosing routes that avoid conflicts, respect bend radius limits (how tightly a wire can bend safely), and are accessible for inspection.

🔗

Connectors & Clamps

Positioning the connectors (the plug-in points at each end of a harness) and clamps (the clips that hold the harness to the structure) in the correct 3D locations.

📄

Manufacturing Output

Producing the documentation the manufacturing team needs — harness drawings, connector tables, and the 3D CATIA model — ready for production.
