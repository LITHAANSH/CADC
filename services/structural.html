What It Involves

Designing what holds everything together.

An aircraft or vehicle doesn't just exist as a collection of individual parts — those parts need a structure to hold them in place and give the product its overall shape and strength. This structure is made up of frames, ribs, beams, and brackets — all of which need to be precisely designed.

For an aircraft, structural work means designing things like: wing ribs (the internal frames inside a wing that give it shape), fuselage frames (the circular rings inside the body of the aircraft), and attachment brackets that hold systems in place.

All of this is designed in CATIA, where we can model the full 3D structure and see how individual parts fit within it — before anything is built.

What we design

✈

Aircraft Structures

Wing ribs (internal frames inside the wing), fuselage frames (the rings inside the body of the aircraft), spars (the main load-carrying beams in a wing), and structural brackets.

🚗

Vehicle Structures

Body structure frames, chassis supports, door frames, and the structural components that give a vehicle its rigidity and strength during use.

🔩

Attachment & Brackets

The brackets and fittings that attach systems, panels, and equipment to the main structure — designed to carry the required loads without adding unnecessary weight.
