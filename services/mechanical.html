What It Involves

From sketch to precise 3D model.

A 3D model (also called a digital model or CAD model) is a computer representation of a physical part — shaped, sized, and detailed to match exactly how it needs to be manufactured. In CATIA, we build this model using a set of design tools that let us create any shape, add holes and features, and define exact dimensions.

Once the model is complete, it becomes the master reference for everything that follows — the technical drawings, the manufacturing process, the quality checks. Getting the 3D model right is what makes everything else work.

We work from whatever you can provide: a pencil sketch, a specification document, a photo of an existing part, or even just a verbal description of what it needs to do.

Types of part we design

🔩

Solid Parts

Parts carved or built from solid material — brackets, housings, fittings, structural members. The most common type of part in aerospace and automotive design.

📄

Sheet Metal Parts

Parts made from thin flat sheets of metal that are bent and formed into shape. Common in aircraft skins, access panels, and brackets. Designed in CATIA's sheet metal tools so they can be "unfolded" for manufacturing.

🔲

Composite Parts

Parts made of layered materials (like carbon fibre) bonded together for strength and light weight. Common in aerospace structures. Each layer's angle and thickness is defined precisely in CATIA.
